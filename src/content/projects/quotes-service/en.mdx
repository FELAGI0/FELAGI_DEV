A storefront for a quotes catalog: it accepts a full snapshot of the catalog and
serves quotes to readers one at a time. All state lives in a single process —
no external database, cache or broker.

## What is inside

- **The snapshot is streamed** — a body of up to 110 MB is parsed chunk by chunk
  and line by line with `orjson`; it is never held in memory whole. A broken
  record is skipped and counted in `dropped`, a broken envelope is a 400.
- **A packed buffer instead of objects** — record bodies live in one `bytearray`
  with a length prefix, an `id → offset` index, and the set of all ids kept
  separately. The storefront therefore still knows an id after its body was
  evicted, and the byte-budgeted LRU acts as a fuse rather than the normal path.
- **A circuit breaker on the catalog** — 5 consecutive transport failures
  (timeout, refused) open the circuit with backoff and jitter; half-open lets one
  probe through. A 503 does *not* open it: that is the catalog answering, not a
  broken link, so `Retry-After` is honoured instead.
- **Honest counters** — `X-Source: LOCAL | CATALOG` on every response, and
  `/stats` reports how much was served from memory, how much was read from the
  catalog and how many bodies were evicted. A 404 from the catalog is not cached,
  and DELETE leaves a tombstone until the next snapshot.
- **Fits in 256 MB of RAM** — peaking around 220 MB on a 340,000-record
  (114 MB) snapshot, with a 0.5 CPU limit and a single uvicorn worker.

## Stack

Python 3.13, FastAPI, uvicorn, orjson, httpx and uvloop. The whole application
is a little under 18 KB of code across four modules; built with `pip` into a
multi-stage image and run as a non-root user.

## Checking it

`scripts/contract_test.py` runs the whole contract against a catalog stub:
import, LOCAL/CATALOG, field boundaries, tombstones and the shape of `/stats`.
